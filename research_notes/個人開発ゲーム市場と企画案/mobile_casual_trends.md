# Mobile Casual Game Trends 2025–2026: Hyper-casual, Hybrid-casual, Casual Puzzle

Research date: 2026-11-09. Method note: WebFetch was blocked for gamedevreports.substack.com and mobidictum.com (network allowlist), so figures below come from search-result snippets of the cited pages and were not read in full. Most numbers are third-party estimates (AppMagic, Sensor Tower, SocialPeta, etc.) and differ by method. Treat them as approximate.

## Q1. Hottest casual genres/mechanics and specific hits (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
The growth in 2025–2026 came from "niche" puzzle subgenres (block puzzle, screw/pin, sort, color-block/jam, and in H1 2026 arrow/escape puzzles) plus hybrid-casual roguelite/idle RPGs (Habby's Archero 2 and Capybara Go). The puzzle hits are dominated by well-funded publishers (Rollic, Voodoo, iKame, Learnings, Miniclip, Hungry Studio) that buy heavy UA, and trends rise and fade within months.

### Cited Findings
**Market context**
- Total mobile game IAP in 2025: ~$81.75B (+1.3% YoY), so growth was concentrated in a few segments. Per secondary coverage of Sensor Tower's State of Gaming 2026, hybrid-casual was the only segment with meaningful IAP growth (+20% YoY, about $4.2B IAP). — [GameDevReports summary of Sensor Tower](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026); [Global Games Forum](https://www.globalgamesforum.com/news/state-of-gaming-heres-what-the-mobile-data-actually-says); [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2026/01/21/in-2025-the-revenue-of-the-mobile-gaming-market-grew-but-only-slightly-analysis)
- Puzzle was among the top genres for IAP growth in 2025. One summary cites puzzle revenue +21% YoY (Sensor Tower Q1 2026). AppMagic data shows casual overall flat to slightly down, so methods conflict. — [GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026); [AppMagic landscape 2026 via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-market-landscape)
- Match-3, Merge and Blast still take about 80% of total puzzle revenue. The newer sort, block and screw subgenres are gaining share. — [Mobidictum](https://mobidictum.com/sort-block-screw-mechanics-puzzle-games-market/); [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/how-niche-subgenres-are-reshaping-the-mobile-puzzle-market/)

### Inferences
- Puzzle mechanics that read instantly in a 3-second ad (sort, unscrew, slide out, arrows escape) win on UA creatives. The subgenre that wins rotates every 6 to 12 months, with arrow puzzles peaking around Apr–Jun 2026 and fading by July.
- Ads-only giants like Block Blast show that pure IAP-free puzzle games can be huge, but only at hundreds of millions of installs.
- Idle/tycoon and merge were not prominent in the 2025–26 growth data I found. They remain big legacy categories, but no specific new-hit data surfaced.

### Gaps
- No verified data was found for Magic Sort, Royal Kingdom, or new idle/tycoon and merge hits in 2025–26.
- I could not read the full AppMagic or Sensor Tower reports because fetches were blocked, so subgenre figures are from snippets.
- I found no data on Q3–Q4 2026 trends after the arrow-puzzle fade.

## Q2. Shift from hyper-casual to hybrid-casual: what changed and why

### Takeaway
Hyper-casual (ads-only, very low CPI, short lifetime) was squeezed by Apple's ATT signal loss, rising CPIs and poor retention. Studios such as Voodoo, Rollic and Habby moved to hybrid-casual, which pairs a hyper-casual-simple core with a casual/midcore meta (progression, boosters, live ops, battle pass) and mixes IAP with ads to reach a long enough LTV to fund UA.

### Cited Findings
- Hybrid-casual is defined as hyper-casual core gameplay plus simplified meta-gameplay from casual or midcore games. Voodoo says this raises retention and LTV. — [GameAnalytics / Voodoo](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/how-voodoo-diversified-and-lowered-game-product-kpis); [PocketGamer.biz interview (Voodoo's Corentin Selz)](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/corentin-selz-voodoo-from-hypercasual-to-hybrid-casual)
- ATT-era signal loss made UA targeting harder and exposed hyper-casual's monetization and retention limits, which pushed the industry toward non-ad monetization. — [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/new-mobile-game-trends); [AppsFlyer hybrid monetization report](https://www.appsflyer.com/open-report/)
- Adjust 2025: average game CPI was $0.38 (2023) and $0.36 (2024). Hyper-casual CPI rose to $0.40, and hybrid-casual CPI nearly doubled to $0.95. — [Adjust Mobile Games Insights 2025 via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-mobile-games-insights-report)
- Hyper-casual IAP held around $0.5B despite falling downloads (Sensor Tower data). — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/gaming/news/what-happened-to-hypercasual-the-markets-evolution-over-the-past-year-5aa76e15-df70-4290-b8c2-60c4e137a30b/)
- Unverified, conflicting growth claims: hybrid-casual IAP +37% in 2024 vs total mobile IAP +4% (Airflux), and hybrid-casual revenue ≈$390M → $733M in 2025 (Applixir). — [Airflux](https://airflux.ai/blog/beyond-ads-hybrid-meta-systems-iap-growth-2026); [Applixir](https://www.applixir.com/?p=3343)
- In June 2025, Deconstructor of Fun noted that Voodoo now measures success by what a game can become over time, not by launch metrics. This contrasts with the hyper-casual era model of low CPI plus solid D1. Voodoo builds about 2,000 prototypes a year. — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/06/02/how-voodoo-builds-hits-four-product-lessons-from-a-2000-prototypes-a-year-machine)
- Example from Habby: Archero 2's revenue rose about 630% after a roguelike/meta overhaul, which shows that meta depth drives revenue. — [iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/1143976939)

### Inferences
- For a solo developer, the "hyper-casual prototype test, then sell to a publisher" route of 2018–2021 is much weaker. Publishers now want games with depth and live-ops potential, which is harder for one person to build.

### Gaps
- The AppsFlyer "+39% hybrid monetization in hypercasual" report was gated, so I could not read the details.

## Q3. Monetization benchmarks, KPIs, costs and store policies for small developers

### Takeaway
Typical KPIs: median D1 ≈ 22% and D7 ≈ 4% across games, with the top 10% at about 40% and 12% (GameAnalytics 2026). Publisher "greenlight" bars have historically been about 40–55% D1 and 13–22% D7. Casual CPI ranges from about $0.14 (Android, global) to $1.41+ (iOS), and is much higher in the US. US rewarded video eCPM is roughly $16–20. Ads-only casual ARPDAU is only about $0.01–0.05. Store fees: 15% commission under $1M on both stores. Google requires a 12-tester/14-day closed test for new personal accounts.

### Cited Findings
**Retention / KPIs**
- GameAnalytics 2026 retention benchmarks: median D1 ≈ 22% (top 10%: ≈ 40%), median D7 ≈ 4% (top 10%: ≈ 12%). Board, card and puzzle games have the highest retention. — [GameAnalytics retention benchmarks 2026 (PDF)](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-20-Mobile_retention_benchmarks_2026.pdf); [2025 GameAnalytics Mobile Gaming Benchmarks (PDF)](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-GameAnalytics-Mobile-Gaming-Benchmarks.pdf)
- Older Voodoo prototype bars (pre-2024): first 55% D1 / 22% D7, later lowered to 45% D1 / 13% D7 / $0.20 CPI. A 2023 Voodoo head-of-publishing target was 45% D1, 15% D7 (20% for puzzle) and 10% D30. — [GameAnalytics / Voodoo](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/how-voodoo-diversified-and-lowered-game-product-kpis); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/corentin-selz-voodoo-from-hypercasual-to-hybrid-casual)
- Older (2022) AppsFlyer band: casual/puzzle D7 on Android was about 9–12%. — [AppAgent](https://appagent.com/?p=61637)
- Case example: Noor Games' Lumbercraft reached 62% D1. — [GameAnalytics](https://gameanalytics.com/blog/how-noor-games-reached-a-d1-retention-of-62-with-the-game-lumbercraft/)

**CPI**
- Liftoff/Singular 2025 casual report (via a Korean summary): average casual CPI was $1.41 on iOS and $0.14 on Android (global blend). Admiral Media lists casual puzzle CPI at $2.50–$6.00, probably for Tier-1/US. These sources conflict. — [FoxData KR](https://foxdata.com/kr/blogs/hypercasual-vs-midcore-how-analytics-strategies-differ-by-game-genre/); [Admiral Media](https://admiral.media/mobile-game-marketing-benchmarks/)
- Adjust: hyper-casual CPI was $0.40 and hybrid-casual $0.95 (2024). — [GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-mobile-games-insights-report)

**Ad monetization (AdMob / AppLovin MAX)**
- US rewarded video average eCPM was $16.49 on Android and $19.63 on iOS. US interstitial was $14.08 (Android) and $14.32 (iOS), though other sources put US interstitials at about $6. — [MAF rewarded ads stats](https://maf.ad/en/blog/rewarded-ads-stats/)
- AdMob ranges: interstitial $5–8 eCPM in Tier-1 regions, rewarded $8–18 globally. — [Playwire](https://www.playwire.com/blog/admob-ecpm-benchmarks-what-publishers-should-expect)
- AppLovin US gaming: rewarded $12–25 and interstitial $6–15. MAX tends to beat AdMob on rewarded and interstitial eCPM in the US, UK and JP, while AdMob does better on banners and App Open ads (vendor blog). — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/applovin-publisher-monetization-guide)
- TopOn H1 2025: in casual games, interstitials are 44% of ad revenue and rewarded video 39%. Banners are 37% of impressions but only 6.5% of revenue. — [Prado](https://prado.co/post/rewarded-video-holds-the-crown-but-interstitial-ads-are-catching-up)
- Appodeal Mobile Casual Benchmarks 2025 (US casual): Merge-3 games served an average of 101.5 rewarded videos per user. — [App Developer Magazine](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/mobile-casual-benchmarks-report-2025/amp/)
- ARPDAU for ads-only casual titles is about $0.01–0.05. Admiral Media's casual-puzzle figure of $1.20–3.00 conflicts with this and likely uses a different definition or population (payers and Tier-1 only). — [Admiral Media KPIs](https://admiral.media/mobile-game-kpis/); [Playio ad revenue calculator](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-ad-revenue-calculator)
- Hybrid monetization stack used by top hybrid-casual games: rewarded ads (continues, boosters), interstitials, IAP boosters/coins, a "remove ads" purchase, and battle-pass/VIP subscriptions. Habby's Archero 2 and Capybara Go! are IAP-heavy RPG metas. — [Deconstructor of Fun on Habby](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/7/31/habbys-hybridcasual-empire-the-template-that-built-a-powerhouse); [Verve on hybrid monetization](https://verve.com/blog/hybrid-monetization-in-casual-games-how-beresnev-strikes-the-right-balance/)

**Store costs / commission**
- Apple Small Business Program: 15% instead of 30% for developers under $1M/year. It requires enrollment, and subscriptions drop to 15% after the first year anyway. — [Appbot](https://appbot.co/blog/app-developers-apple-google-small-business-programs/); [PricePush](https://pricepush.app/blog/apple-small-business-program)
- Google Play: 15% on the first $1M/year (requires an account group or enrollment), and 15% on auto-renewing subscriptions from day one. Possible change: one source reports that from June 30, 2026, Google charges a 10% service fee plus a 5% billing fee on the first $1M in the US, UK and EEA. This is not confirmed by a primary source. — [SplitMetrics](https://splitmetrics.com/blog/google-play-apple-app-store-fees/); [Magora](https://magora-systems.com/app-store-small-business-program/)
- Registration fees: Google Play charges $25 once and the Apple Developer Program charges $99/year. These are standard published fees, but I did not re-verify them this session. Official pages: [Apple Developer Program](https://developer.apple.com/programs/) and [Google Play Console signup](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435).
- Google Play: personal developer accounts created after Nov 13, 2023 must run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days before applying for production access. Internal testing does not count. Production review usually takes up to about 7 days, and a rejection restarts the closed test. Organization accounts (which need a D-U-N-S number) are reportedly exempt. Budget about 3 weeks for a first launch. — [Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-play-app-testing-requirement-3510580); [Level Up "12-tester tax"](https://levelup.gitconnected.com/the-12-tester-tax-google-plays-closed-testing-rule-is-quietly-killing-solo-devs-2cea83c2e338)

**AI-generated content policies**
- Google Play AI-Generated Content policy: it applies to apps that generate content via AI (text, voice or image prompts). Such apps must prevent restricted or deceptive content and include in-app reporting or flagging. Apps that only display AI-made assets are outside its scope. A July 2025 update added a best-practices link. — [Google Play Help 14094294](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14094294); [Policy updates](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16296680)
- Google tightened GenAI rules on non-consensual intimate content (Aug 2026 blog). — [Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/08/ensuring-safety-genai-preventing-non-consensual-intimate-content.html?hl=es_419&m=1)
- Apple: guideline 4.3 (spam / copycat apps) and 4.2 (minimum functionality) are the relevant risks for low-effort or templated or AI-generated apps. One article covers rejections of AI-built apps. — [vp0 blog](https://vp0.com/blogs/app-store-rejection-spam-design-ai-fix); [vp0 AI approval](https://vp0.com/blogs/apple-app-store-ai-approval-flutterflow-cursor)

### Inferences
- Rough math: at an ads-only ARPDAU of $0.02–0.05, 1,000 DAU earns about $20–50/day. Earning $3,000/month needs roughly 2,000–5,000 DAU in sustained Tier-1-weighted traffic, which is hard without paid UA.
- Using AI-generated art in a game is not banned by either store. The Google policy targets in-app generative features. Rejection risk comes from "spam/minimum functionality", not from AI use as such.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative, first-party AdMob or AppLovin eCPM benchmark for casual games in 2025–26. All figures are from vendor blogs.
- I found no explicit Apple guideline text on AI-generated game assets.
- The Google June 2026 fee restructure is unconfirmed.

## Q4. Indie/solo success stories, Block Blast origin, failure rates

### Takeaway
The big casual mobile hits of 2025–26 were not solo projects. Block Blast came from a team with web mini-game traffic expertise that grew to nearly 1,000 staff, and the puzzle hits come from Rollic, Voodoo, iKame, Learnings and Miniclip using heavy paid UA. Solo successes driven by TikTok exist mostly on PC/Steam or in premium and daily-puzzle niches. Revenue is extremely concentrated, and most indie games earn very little.

### Cited Findings
- **Block Blast origin.** Founder Liu Xuezheng was a core member of 7k7k, a Chinese web mini-game portal, so the team brought ad and traffic-operation skills. Hungry Studio was founded in 2021 according to PC Games Insider (one blog says Hong Kong, but most sources say Beijing). It grew from a "small workshop" to nearly 1,000 employees by the end of 2025, and Tencent reportedly planned a minority stake. The release year of Block Blast is inconsistent across sources (2019, 2022 and 2023 are all cited). It had over 216M installs in 2024 alone. Its success is credited to AI-assisted A/B testing and a focus on long-term players. — [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/de/p/3706617203732616); [PC Games Insider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/company/26257/hungry-studio/); [Global Games Forum](https://www.globalgamesforum.com/news/how-block-blast-shattered-records-with-ai-a-b-testing-and-a-bold-bet-on-long-term-players); [Block Blast official](https://blockblast.com/about-us)
- **Small studio example.** Waffle, a daily word game studio, is "the latest small dev to find a big audience" on mobile. — [Mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/daily-word-game-studio-waffle-is-the-latest-small-dev-to-find-a-big-audience)
- **Solo, mostly PC.** Storyteller (Daniel Benmergui) took off on TikTok about a year after launch, which he called "spontaneous". Balatro (solo developer LocalThunk) reached the iPhone Top Paid list in 2025. Sledding Game (solo) sold 100K copies in 5 days after posting development clips. — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/puzzle/indie-dev-thought-their-puzzle-game-would-just-die-out-but-a-weird-sales-curve-and-tiktok-virality-led-to-great-success-a-year-after-launch); [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/creativity-over-cost-how-viral-indies-of-2025-succeeded)
- **Indie earnings disclosure.** The Tiny Terraces creator shared revenue numbers. ### Inferences
- A solo beginner can realistically ship to both stores and earn pocket money to a small side income in a niche: daily puzzle, a distinctive premium puzzle, or a clone variant with a twist. Competing head-on in sort, screw, block or arrow puzzles against UA-funded publishers is unlikely to pay off without paid UA, because organic store discovery is weak.
- The more realistic paths are (a) building an audience through development clips on TikTok/Shorts, (b) web portals (Poki, CrazyGames) to validate retention first, or (c) pitching a prototype with strong D1 and CPI numbers to a publisher.

## Q5. Wrapping HTML5 games as mobile apps (Capacitor, etc.)

### Takeaway
It is viable for 2D casual puzzle games. WebView performance is generally sufficient, and Capacitor has maintained AdMob plugins (a new Capawesome plugin built on Google's Next-Gen Mobile Ads SDK launched in Sept 2026). The risks are ad-induced frame drops, Android fragmentation, and Apple guideline 4.2 ("repackaged website") rejections. A real offline game with native features passes much more easily than a URL wrapper.

### Cited Findings
- Construct's (2016, old) benchmark found WebView performance on par with desktop browsers, with problems on old Android devices and GPU blacklists. Capacitor and Cordova share similar underlying WebView behavior. — [Construct blog](https://www.construct.net/en/blogs/construct-official-blog-1/great-html5-gaming-856); [Construct forum](https://construct.net/en/forum/construct-3/general-discussion-7/moving-away-cordova-183821)
- Phaser 3 + Capacitor Android reports include white screens, texture rendering failures and orientation issues. — [Phaser Discourse](https://phaser.discourse.group/t/phaser-3-capacitor-android-apk-white-screens-texture-rendering-failures-and-orientation-issues/15571)
- Anecdotes of the AdMob plugin causing FPS drops or stutter at startup. — [Google Groups](https://groups.google.com/g/google-admob-ads-sdk/c/PsjvXumeL9M)
- Plugins: `@capacitor-community/admob`; Capawesome's Capacitor AdMob plugin (Sept 2026, Next-Gen SDK, with banner, interstitial, rewarded and app-open formats); `cordova-plugin-admob-nextgen` (also works with Capacitor). — [Capawesome](https://capawesome.io/docs/blog/announcing-the-capacitor-admob-plugin/); [ecosyste.ms](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fswaplab-engine%2Fcordova-plugin-admob-nextgen)
- Capacitor game wrapping guide. — [Abratabia](https://abratabia.com/native-wrappers/capacitor-games.php); [Playgama porting guide](https://playgama.com/blog/uncategorized/guide-to-porting-html5-games-for-mobile-success/)
- Apple 4.2 Minimum Functionality: an app must offer "features, content, and UI that elevate it beyond a repackaged website". Reviewers may test offline (airplane mode). Fixes include offline support, native features and native navigation. — [MobiLoud](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/app-store-review-guidelines-webview-wrapper); [Capgo 2026](https://capgo.app/blog/how-to-pass-app-store-review-2026/); [Apple Dev Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/835422)

### Inferences
- Bundle all assets locally (no remote URL), support offline play, and add native touches such as haptics, Game Center or Play Games leaderboards, local notifications and IAP. A self-contained HTML5 puzzle game is then a legitimate app, not a "website wrapper".
- Integrate ads early and profile on low-end Android devices. For heavier physics or 3D, Unity or Godot are safer.

- I found no game-specific data on Apple 4.2 rejection rates for Capacitor games.
- I found no 2025–26 benchmark comparing Capacitor WebView FPS with native engines.
